Reference 1 is the front of a tall carved wooden fireplace surround as a clay maquette. Reference 2 is the museum's photograph of the real object seen from an angle, which shows its side and how far each part stands out from the wall. Draw this exact object, in the same light grey matte clay and light as reference 1, in pure side profile: the camera is level and at exactly ninety degrees to the front, looking at the side that reference 2 shows. Its front faces the left edge of the picture. Its back is flat and plain, because it stands against a wall: the right edge of the silhouette is one straight vertical line from top to bottom. The object is shallow: about one seventh as deep as it is tall. The lower part, the arch around the firebox with its twisting plant-stem support, stands out furthest; the mantel shelf with its small draped attendant figure overhangs a little; the tall panel above the shelf is thin and set back against the wall, with its carved flowers at the top. Do not draw a three-quarter view: no part of the front may be seen face-on. Keep every height of reference 1. Show the whole object, centred, filling about three quarters of the frame height. Plain seamless white background with no horizon line. No cast shadow, no reflections, no text.
