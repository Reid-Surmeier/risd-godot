# Independent anonymous display review — A/B/C

**Verdict: INCONCLUSIVE; retain the control. No variant earns selection.** Written before any key, mode, code, log, or sibling-folder inspection. The packet does not demonstrate a clear improvement in two scenes without regression.

## Evidence and method

Reviewed only the54 PNG/WebM files in `/tmp/gallery-display-140/blind-copy/`, plus the supplied house screenshot and saved Animal Crossing57:32 image `docs/research/gallery-final-render-overlay/video-3452.png`. The house remains the composition/scale reference;57:32 supplies lighting evidence, not a requirement to copy that museum's layout or character size.

Inspected all30 PNGs: A/B/C at1600 and720 for entry, warm, warm-held, art and white. After visual comparison, decoded RGB comparison confirmed that **all three complete PNG images are pixel-identical in each of the ten matched scene/size sets**. Both A/B and A/C comparisons have zero differing pixels, including the full shell, not merely a selected crop. This is direct image evidence; it does not establish why the supplied variants produced identical stills.

Inspected actual recorded motion through extracted frame sequences from all24 WebMs: twelve samples at6fps from each clip's first approximately two seconds, with sample indices2,5,8 and11 compared visually side by side. This covers96 viewed motion frames across four scenes and both sizes. I also inspected native-size still crops and nearest-neighbour2× detail crops of art, doorway and floor. Temporary inspection sheets are under `/tmp/gallery-display-blind-review/`; no source pixels were changed. I did not perceive uninterrupted video playback, so this is sampled motion evidence and cannot certify the absence of subtle high-frequency shimmer.

## Motion qualifications and shared visible issue

The recorded walking pose and exact camera position differ slightly between variants at corresponding sample indices. For example, C's early warm/art samples show a different leg phase from A/B. Those differences can alter moving edges and compression; they are not proof of a display-filter improvement. I found no scene-specific, repeatable superiority sufficient to meet the two-scene rule.

In all three white-room clips, after returning to the gallery, the bottom of the active picture exposes an alternating parquet/near-black zigzag edge. It is visible at both sizes in samples5,8 and11. This is a shared rendered composition/geometry observation; the packet gives no basis to attribute it to one display variant. It should be checked separately if that return view is intended for ordinary navigation.

No mode key was opened or inferred for this verdict. Keep the existing control under the stated rule. These artifacts cannot support a claim that one tested treatment improves the finish; the identical stills warrant verifying that a future intervention actually changes the captured render before evaluating its quality.
