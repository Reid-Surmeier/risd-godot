**Dithering, crawl, and motion**

A’s fine floor pattern changes conspicuously across consecutive moving frames. This produces the strongest impression of texture activity, particularly in the 1600 entry and warm views. Some fine texture becomes indistinct and then resolves again as the view moves.

B moderates that activity while preserving the recognizable parquet structure. C is smoother still, but that smoothness also reaches painting interiors, gilt ornament, and character outlines. Consequently, “less busy” does not consistently mean “better.”

Bright, thin frame ornament remains susceptible to changing pixel-level appearance in all three labels. The samples do not establish that any label eliminates crawl. At 720, the differences are substantially smaller.

The Animal Crossing house reference combines soft edges with distinct material patterns, strong color separation, and readable illumination. The museum reference has a bright ceiling and trim, cooler blue walls, a comparatively pale floor, and a strongly dark doorway. None clearly advances the museum lighting match.

The preserved reference sheets retain recognizable tile, foliage, and architectural patterns despite their limited resolution. They do not make a strong case for either A’s pervasive fine patterning or C’s additional softness.

**Evidence limits and decision**

1. These are encoded browser recordings. Compression can obscure fine texture or contribute to changing pixel patterns; I cannot isolate rendering-induced crawl from capture artifacts.
2. Motion was assessed through sampled and consecutive frames, not uninterrupted real-time playback. This supports detail comparisons more strongly than judgments about perceived smoothness.
3. Timing and camera positions are not perfectly matched, particularly in the 1600 art clips. I did not treat different poses or framing as label defects.
4. The white-room clips leave the room early and later return. Its stills carry more weight; entry, art, and warm provide the sustained moving-scene evidence.
5. The references differ in composition, resolution, and capture treatment. They support qualitative comparisons, not an exact sharpness or color target.

**B offers the best apparent compromise, but only the 1600 white scene provides a straightforward improvement. Elsewhere, the changes are small or exchange detail for smoothness. A and C also carry visible drawbacks. The required two-scene, no-material-regression threshold is therefore not met.**
