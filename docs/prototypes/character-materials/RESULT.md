The previous `mMouth` Mix-red cutout removes its central black rectangle, but not the side bands. The black side bands are therefore principally the scratch converter's **opaque black placeholder on the authored Paint mesh**, not the mouth overlay alone.

The original archive directly served [`PlayerBody/mPaint_Alb.png`](https://archive.org/download/acnh-2.0.0-models/ACNH_2.0.0_Exported_Model_DAE%2BPNG.7z/Model/PlayerBody.Nin_NX_NVN/mPaint_Alb.png): 128×128 flat gray RGBA, SHA-256 `6919fe70b106f53d55f7a51d0509710d3a53293fa93580fe5a4fbcc915d350ee`. This is a **diagnostic green for one numeric symptom, not a visual pass**: the lower face remains a visibly different gray from the upper face and center. A material-ID render proves the actual geometry regions: `mCheek` covers the upper face, `mPaint` the lower sides, and `mMouth` the central lower patch. A lit/unlit pair shows the source-color cheek becomes much darker under the scratch Cycles treatment, while unlit source color is light. Hair shadow, overlay shadow, frontal fill, and cheek alpha probes did not remove the seams. Offsetting the cheek mesh moved its dark marks to the wrong face region. No single-source fix supports calling this faithful yet.

Inference: the exported DAEs plus generic PBR conversion omit a source-specific face compositing/tint rule, so further shader reconstruction needs source-channel and UV validation rather than arbitrary recoloring. The brown/white face in the current style trial still fails the owner's standard. The next useful probe is projected UV/depth ordering of **all** face-bearing meshes and a per-pixel material-ID/depth pass, not another tint guess.
