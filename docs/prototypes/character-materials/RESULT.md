## One-variable results

With the same camera, light, and crop (`200x88+200+214`, gray threshold 15%), the rejected lower-face dark-pixel fraction is `0.242216`. Hiding only `Paint__mPaint` yields `0.119716`; hiding cheek, mouth, or softmesh alone yields `0.244489`, `0.229091`, and `0.242216`. The previous `mMouth` Mix-red cutout removes its central black rectangle, but not the side bands. The black side bands are therefore principally the scratch converter's **opaque black placeholder on the authored Paint mesh**, not the mouth overlay alone.

The original archive directly served [`PlayerBody/mPaint_Alb.png`](https://archive.org/download/acnh-2.0.0-models/ACNH_2.0.0_Exported_Model_DAE%2BPNG.7z/Model/PlayerBody.Nin_NX_NVN/mPaint_Alb.png): 128×128 flat gray RGBA, SHA-256 `6919fe70b106f53d55f7a51d0509710d3a53293fa93580fe5a4fbcc915d350ee`. Replacing the scratch black with that source map and applying the cheek Mix alpha drops the numeric fraction to `0.0889205`. This is a **diagnostic green for one numeric symptom, not a visual pass**: the lower face remains a visibly different gray from the upper face and center. A material-ID render proves the actual geometry regions: `mCheek` covers the upper face, `mPaint` the lower sides, and `mMouth` the central lower patch. A lit/unlit pair shows the source-color cheek becomes much darker under the scratch Cycles treatment, while unlit source color is light. Hair shadow, overlay shadow, frontal fill, and cheek alpha probes did not remove the seams. Offsetting the cheek mesh moved its dark marks to the wrong face region. No single-source fix supports calling this faithful yet.

The [ACNH model-modding guide](https://acnhmodding.github.io/creating-mods/creating-npc-model/) documents the original DAE/texture export route, low-poly body-part naming and per-part materials; it is **not** a specification of the game's packed-channel face shader. Inference: the exported DAEs plus generic PBR conversion omit a source-specific face compositing/tint rule, so further shader reconstruction needs source-channel and UV validation rather than arbitrary recoloring. The brown/white face in the current style trial still fails the owner's standard. No blind review or Godot acceptance was requested for a knowingly failing render; the next candidate needs coherent face material first.

Follow-up control: even when the archived cheek, paint and skin albedos are passed through the same unlit skin-palette ramp, the upper face, lower sides and central mouth patch remain visibly different. Forcing the ramp endpoints to the same color and making all three materials opaque does not remove the bands; hiding the cheek mesh in that setup does not remove them either. The remaining mismatch therefore cannot be attributed to a single albedo color or light source. The next useful probe is projected UV/depth ordering of **all** face-bearing meshes and a per-pixel material-ID/depth pass, not another tint guess. This control is intentionally not an asset candidate.

That depth probe identified a second concrete cause and corrected the earlier hair-occlusion inference. At upper-face screen pixels `(250,215)` and `(350,215)`, the rejected-view camera ray meets `Body__mEye`, then two `Hair__mHair` surfaces **behind it**, then `Body__mSkin`; at lower cheek pixels `(250,265)`/`(350,265)` it meets `Paint__mPaint`, `Body__mCheek`, then `Body__mSkin`. At the mouth center `(300,265)` it meets `Body__mMouth` before skin. The scratch converter had made the authored eye and mouth planes transparent wherever their markings were absent, revealing the hair behind the eye plane and differently shaded skin behind the mouth plane. The hair does **not** geometrically occlude an opaque face there. Moving the hair rig backward was a wrong repair: it exposed more scalp. The correct face-specific trial makes those planes opaque by compositing their source markings over matching skin colour: source eye albedo alpha for the eye, and `1 - mMouth_Mix.red > 0.5` for the mouth.
