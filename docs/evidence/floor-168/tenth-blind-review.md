## Exact findings

1. **Major — pattern construction.** The photos show staggered, interlocking rectangular boards with distinct end joints. The candidate reads predominantly as continuous chevron bands, with aligned zigzag meeting lines. Individual rectangular boards are difficult to follow. This affects the broad pattern, beyond differences in viewpoint.
2. **Major — board variation and grain.** Candidate boards have subdued beige, gray and pink variation, but large areas merge into evenly striped fields. The fine grain is unusually straight, dense and repetitive, especially in Web images 9–10. The photos show more irregular grain and clearer differences between neighboring boards. Beside the detailed frames, the floor feels procedural and underdeveloped.
3. **Moderate — sheen and surface depth.** The reference has a broad, soft daylight sheen while retaining visible wood structure. The candidate appears comparatively matte and uniformly hazy, with little visible variation in surface response between boards. The soft bench shadows ground the scene, but do not establish a convincing finished-oak surface.
4. **Moderate — native/Web parity.** Layout, board colors, lighting and shadows appear closely matched. Sharpness does not: native images 3–6 are visibly softer than corresponding Web images 7–10. Grain and joints lose definition in native, including at 1600. The same softness affects frames and trim, suggesting a capture or rendering difference rather than necessarily a floor-specific defect.
5. **Strengths and limits.** The pale oak palette, restrained seams, diagonal rhythm and perspective form a coherent room surface; there are no conspicuous gaps or gross texture discontinuities in these views. Board scale is plausible, though the unclear end joints weaken that judgment. Exact physical dimensions, oak species, texture resolution, filtering, material settings, reflection behavior during movement and the cause of native softness **cannot be determined from these stills**.
